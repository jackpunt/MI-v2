

individual cells (have little functional value)

clusters of cells can interoperate to form organioids, and function.
such clusters can provide the basis pattern recognition.
clusters or arrays of such clusters can provide memory and prediction.
the memory is approximate and the predictions are error prone, but it begins.
extended and interconected clusters and arrays can do feedback and selection or amplification which provides error correction of a sort, and improve accuracy of predictions.

Still there are competing predictions, current events/stimulation correlates with multiple previous states, activating multiple memories and past outcomes (or synthetic/predicted outcomes).
Choices must be made, a concensus decision on a course of action.

A next level of oragnization creates a "chooser", some selection of attention to a POV, a focus on certain aspects of the situation, a selection or prioritization of the input/stimulation/recollection, deciding which set of recognizers/predictors is the most relevant to the expected/predicted/(desired?) future outcome. Although there can be multiple assesments and interpreetation of the present, and multiple expectations/predictions of the future/outcomes of each action/response, the organism must choose a _single_ course of action: the glands will secrete or not, a commitment for the muscles coordinate to either fight/flight/freeze/feed/fuck. It does not work to have individual subsystems each taking a different course of action. Evolution and survival will select for coordinated and commited activity; the alternative is for the organism to be simply "food".

So there must be something in the layers of cellular organization that evolves to be the 'decider'.

Goes back to the hierarchy of organization and levels of abstraction or order of magnitude of scale.
At higher levels, there are emergent properties that 'override' or 'redefine' the organization/definition/operation of the system. If it _possible_ to use reductionism to explain the higher level in terms of the lower level, but becomes difficult and 'un-economical' (in terms of insight per analytical effort) to continue at the lower level. the emergent properties are the properties of interest, the lower level 'implementation details' become less useful, and in some cases totally irrelevant 

See also a discussion of _virtual machines_; the orthogonality of what is being achieved or 'computed' from the mechanism or materials being used. a computer can be build from electronics or hydraulics or mechanial gears and ratches, and still produce the same results. Although: the choice of technology will affect how it system scales! the emergent properties of a steam-powered mechanical computer will be vastly different from the emergent properties of a electrically powered, transistor-
based computer.
